# Chapter 1— The Call to Duty
The air that morning buzzed against my skin — sharp, bright, the way it gets when the field is about to change. I was twenty-two. My sisters and I had just graduated from mageic school, our conduits finally waking for real. Before that, our wands were just symbols. Pretty things. Promises. Nothing that could hold a spell.

My conduit was a steel bracelet on my right arm, cold even in summer. Blackened by a magic older and heavier than anything Ethyria teaches. I didn’t understand it then. I only knew it felt like a warning. Or a memory.

That same magic had tried to claim me twenty years earlier. I was too young to understand what was happening — only that the darkness swallowed my parents and reached for me next. I ran. I was scared, but something in me refused to stop. Maybe it was instinct. Maybe it was the first flicker of the dusk-light I would come to trust.

When Rivten found me, I knew I was safe before I knew his name. Later, when Korrin and Kira joined our family, the smudge on my arm deepened until it covered me completely. They brought light into places I didn’t realize were dark.

---
"Mira, you have a guest." Korrin sidled to my side as I sat on the ground beside my mother as she groomed herself, her cat way of life on full display. As I got up to attend to this guest, my mom put a paw on my arm, as if to say, "_Stay, girl._"
“I’ll be back, *m’lao‑hrr‑se*,” I said, the worry already tightening along my ribs. The name‑sound slipped out before I could stop it — the one piece of Instinct‑speak my parents and their generation taught me that I never managed to let go of. I’d abandoned the rest years ago, but my parents’ name‑sounds stayed rooted in me, rising whenever I wasn’t sure of my next step.

My mother heard it immediately. Her ears flicked, her long tail sweeping once across the floor as she stood. The worry in her bright eyes mirrored mine.

“I’m… not sure about this guy,” I admitted, making a sharp little gesture of frustration toward her. She answered with a low purr, warm and steady, the kind she used when she wanted me to feel her beside me.

“Do… you want me to join you?” she asked in her careful English.

“No, aunt Milao. We’ve got it, thanks.”
Korrin smiled as she said it, using her own sound‑name for my mother — the one shaped like the way her birth‑mother called her. It always softened Milao’s ears.

My mother settled back down, though her tail-tip twitched. She didn’t like my answer, but she knew I wasn’t going to budge.

Korrin and I headed downstairs from our room, where my mother and I lay. Callendor always felt too big when you were walking toward something unknown — the kind of big that made your pulse echo in places you didn’t want it to. The stone curves caught every sound and stretched it, and the high windows let in so much light it felt like the building was watching you.

My aunt and Elder Anna designed it that way. A place meant to hold power, yes — but also a place that reminded you you were stepping into someone else’s current. Every hallway had a way of making you feel smaller or sharper, depending on what you carried in your chest. Today, it made me feel exposed.

Korrin walked close enough that our shoulders brushed now and then, her field steady and warm. She always did that when she sensed my dusk-light tightening. She didn’t say anything — she never needed to — but the quiet weight of her presence kept my breath from going shallow.

The stairs curved downward in a slow spiral, and each step felt like it was taking us deeper into something I hadn’t agreed to yet. The air changed as we descended, cooler, sharper, like the building itself was bracing. I tried not to read too much into it, but my instincts were already awake, flickering along my right arm.

By the time we reached the lower floor, the whole place felt too still. Callendor was never silent — not with a house full of mages and family — but now the quiet pressed in like a held breath.

A man stood in front of the kitchen counter, dressed in silvers and grey. Not the kind you see in Ethyria — not mage robes, not travel leathers, not the always–too–casual attire Danaéans seemed to like — but something too smooth, too deliberate. He looked like he’d stepped out of a half-remembered realm, the kind that only shows up in dreams you don’t admit you’ve had. Too still. Too polished. Like he didn’t breathe unless someone was watching.

Korrin’s field tightened beside me, a warm, protective curl. Mine flickered in response, dusk-light rising along my right arm in a thin, warning pulse. The air around him felt wrong — not dangerous, exactly, but… misaligned. Like he was standing half a step out of the world we were in.

“Glad to finally meet you, Mira.”

He said my name like he’d practiced it.
Like he’d been waiting for this moment longer than I’d been alive.

My stomach dropped.
My instincts didn’t like that at all.

He knew my name.
Either he was important… or he was insane.

I decided on the second one.

Korrin didn’t disagree.


---
“I wish for you to return to the Shadow Fields,” he said, as if he were offering me a cup of tea instead of a death sentence. “It will be tough. But I feel you three will endure the trials. Within the Fields lies a treasure greater than this family.”

He smiled.

It wasn’t a warm smile.
It wasn’t even a polite one.

It felt like poison — sweet on the surface, sharp underneath. The kind of smile someone uses when they’ve already decided what you’re going to do, and they’re just waiting for you to catch up.

My dusk-light flared along my right arm, a thin pulse of warning. Korrin shifted beside me, her field tightening in a way that said she felt it too.

He talked about the Shadow Fields like they were a place you could choose to walk into. Like they were a puzzle. A challenge. A prize.

He didn’t understand them at all.

Or worse — he did.

Either way, the smile didn’t belong on his face. Not when he said my name. Not when he said Fields. Not when he said treasure greater than this family.

That was the moment I knew something was wrong with him.
Not dangerous in the way monsters are dangerous.
Dangerous in the way people are when they believe they’re right.

And that’s always the harder kind.

And yet something in me tightened, low and certain. I felt like I had to accept. Not because he was right — he wasn’t — but because the danger he carried in his voice felt like it already had my name on it. Like it had been circling me for years, waiting for me to stop pretending I couldn’t feel it.

And I knew my sisters wouldn’t leave my side. They never had. They were too proud, too kind, too protective — the kind of protective that settles into your bones before you realize you’re leaning on it.

Especially Kira.

Kira, who moved like a shield even when she pretended she wasn’t paying attention.
Kira, whose field always curled around mine the moment danger breathed in our direction.
Kira, who would walk into the Shadow Fields without hesitation if it meant I didn’t have to walk alone.

The thought tightened something in my chest — not fear, not exactly, but the ache that comes when you realize the people you love will follow you into places they shouldn’t have to go.

Korrin’s shoulder brushed mine again, steady and warm.
Kira wasn’t even in the room, and I could still feel her loyalty like a hand at my back.

This danger might have been mine…
but I knew they’d claim it with me anyway.

She came downstairs already tight in the shoulders. Not angry — just wound up in that way she gets when something’s pressed too close to her. I don’t know what he said to her, but the silver man was the last person she spoke to before she came down, and her field felt… scraped. Like she was still holding the echo of him.

I can’t be sure it was him. I just know how she looks when someone’s gotten under her skin, and this felt like that. Her lightpatch was pulled too wide, and she was walking like she was bracing for something she didn’t want to deal with.

So maybe it was him. Maybe not. But whatever it was, she didn’t come down bristling at us. She came down already carrying something, and directing it at the guy who regardless was hurting me. 

"What's going on?" The man managed — impossibly — to stop the fire of the sixteen‑year‑old who had only just found her flame. And he did it with a single word.

“Kira.”

Not shouted.
Not whispered.
Just spoken — clean, precise, like he was naming a truth he had no right to know.

Kira’s fire snapped inward so fast I felt the air shift. Her whole field tightened, not in fear but in that razor‑still way she gets when something hits her instincts before her mind can catch up. Her heat folded in on itself, coiled, waiting.

Korrin inhaled sharply beside me.
Even she felt it.

My dusk‑light flickered along my right arm, a warning pulse I couldn’t ignore. The name shouldn’t have come from him. It shouldn’t have landed the way it did — like he’d reached straight into the part of Kira that listens only to instinct and lineage.

Kira didn’t move.
Didn’t speak.
Didn’t blink.

But the room changed around her.

And the man — the stranger — watched her with the calm of someone who expected this reaction. Someone who had been waiting for it.

That was the part that made my stomach drop.

He didn’t just know my name.
He knew hers.

And that meant he knew far too much.

"Better yet, _who_ are you?" Kira continued, her fire tempered but not extinguished. 
"Ah, yes, all you need to know is I am on your side."

“I trust him,” I said, though the words tasted wrong the moment they left me. “He looks like my parents… before they went dark. Before the Fields twisted them.”

My smudge flared—sharp, bright, warning—like it wanted to speak for me.

“And he knows things. About us. Too many things.”
I swallowed, feeling the pulse in my arm climb higher, hotter.
“Don’t you think he’s from the Shadow Fields? Am I the only one who sees it?”

Their faces stayed steady. Calm. Unmoved.

Of course they didn’t feel it.
Of course they didn’t hear the way the Fields hum behind his voice.

They didn’t grow up in that place.
They didn’t learn to read danger by the temperature of their own skin.

I exhaled, slow.

“Right. You wouldn’t know it the way I do.”

The smudge throbbed again—agreement, or memory, or both.

"We trust you, Mira. It's your decision and we'll go with you." Kira said this with absolute certainty. Korrin, the littlest of us three, put her head on my shoulder, and asked, "So, what's your choice, sister?"

Kira’s certainty steadied me. Korrin’s head on my shoulder anchored me. For a breath, everything inside me went quiet enough to hear what was actually mine.

I let my hand rest over Korrin’s, felt the warmth of her field, felt Kira’s trust like a shield settling into place beside me.

“My choice?” I exhaled.
The smudge pulsed once—clear, insistent.

“We go forward,” I said. “With him. With caution, but with me leading.”

I lifted my chin, letting the decision root itself in my chest.

“If there’s danger, I’ll feel it first. If there’s truth, I’ll see it. And you’ll be with me.”

The smudge steadied.
So did I.

“That’s my answer.”

---
It had already been a long day when the silver man turned back to us, brushing the last bus away with a flick of his hand.
“We walk,” he said. “It won’t matter soon whether it’s dark or not.”

Korrin shivered beside me—thin shirt, bare shoulders, the pre‑dusk chill already biting at her.
“How long will it take?” she asked, voice small but steady.

“As long as it takes,” he answered, too casually for someone who knew exactly what that meant.

Korrin didn’t argue, but her field snapped tight. I felt it before I saw it—my smudge bristling in immediate response, heat rising under my skin. Thirteen years with her, and I could read her emotional temperature faster than breath.

“You should have dressed well, Korrin,” Kira said, half‑scolding, half‑sigh.

Korrin whipped her glare toward her, indignation bright as a flare.
“I was expecting to just visit near Callendor!” she shot back.

Her shoulderless shirt left her smudge exposed—the long, jagged mark from the rabid sandcat that tore her life open and drove her into ours. Thirteen years later, it still pulsed with the memory of that night.

And as the silver man started walking, that smudge of hers—hers and mine—told me the same thing:

This path wasn’t going to be simple.
And he knew more than he was saying.

---
"“Does this guy know where we are?” Korrin snapped, her green hair a wild halo now that all her braids had fallen loose. Four hours since the bus, and every one of us was frayed at the edges.

The silver man stopped, turned, and swept a hand around us.
“Look. Breathe. Mira—do you recognize this place?”

The forest pressed close, thick with shadow and scent. And underneath it all… something familiar. A hum I hadn’t felt in years.

My breath caught.
“Is this… where I found my father? Twenty years ago?”
My hand lifted on its own.
“I… remember this tree.”

I stepped forward and touched the bark.

The world narrowed to the warmth beneath my palm.
I longed for my flower dress—the one that steadied me when I was small, when my magic was louder than my voice. This was the tree I hid behind. The tree that taught me how to listen to myself. The first place I ever felt safe.

My tree.

A smile broke over me before I could stop it.

“This is where I met my father,” I said softly. “He waited for me to come to him. He didn’t pull me out—he let me choose him. And when I did… he held me the way I needed. He gave me a home. My mother did too.”

I pressed my forehead to the bark.

“To see it again… I’m honored.”

The silver man’s voice drifted in behind me.
“This is to remind you what you’re fighting for. It will get harder from here. And this tree—”

But I wasn’t listening anymore.

I knelt, scooped dirt into my hands, and began shaping it over the roots—packing it, smoothing it, forming a tunnel the way I used to when I was little. Kira lit a small flame in her palm without needing to be asked, and I used the heat to bake the clay firm.

A marker. A promise. A way back.

So I would never lose my tree again. 

---
The fog had been with us since the bus, but now it thickened—heavy, swallowing sound, swallowing light. A familiar dread slid up my spine, the kind that comes from places with no sky. Darkness. Danger. Memory.

My smudge contracted so fast it hurt.

Korrin felt it first; she always does. She moved in close, her field brushing mine in a steadying sweep. Kira followed a heartbeat later, heat rising off her like a shield snapping into place. They didn’t say anything—they didn’t need to. They knew I was faltering.

The silver man reached out and touched my shoulder.

My breath hitched.

Before he could press further, Kira’s flame flared between us—bright, sharp, protective. A warning line drawn in fire.

He froze.
Good.

Because whatever was in this fog… whatever was waking up inside me… it wasn’t something he got to touch.

It was memory—raw, tidal, overwhelming.
My one‑year‑old self surged up inside my twenty‑one‑year‑old body, terrified and reaching for safety that wasn’t here yet. The forest stretched too tall, the fog pressed too close, and every sound felt like it came from a world built for giants.

My smudge contracted so sharply it stole my breath.

Korrin closed in on my left, Kira on my right, both of them bracing me without a word. Their fields wrapped around mine, trying to anchor me, but the child inside me was already crying, already drowning out everything else.

The silver man stepped forward again, hand lifting toward me.

Kira’s flame snapped up between us—bright, protective, furious.

But I barely saw it.

The ground tilted. My knees buckled. The world went white around the edges.

And then I was falling—
not gracefully, not slowly—
just collapsing under the weight of a memory too young for a body too grown.

I fainted before I even hit the forest floor.

---

“Gods, you’re so heavy,” drifted toward me first—Korrin’s voice, unmistakable, half‑complaint and half‑worry braided together like she couldn’t decide which one she meant.

I groaned.
“Thanks. I’m already pretty skinny,” I muttered, and her smudge fluttered—broadening in that warm, embarrassed way she gets when she’s trying not to laugh at me.

When I finally blinked the world into focus, everyone was there, crowded over my shoulder like I’d been out long enough to make them consider taking shifts.

“Don’t be silly,” I said, sharper than I intended. My voice had that brittle, overextended edge it gets when I’m running on fumes. And there was this strange sensation—like the air above my head had texture. A faint coolness, a shape that didn’t belong to hair or sweat or anything I could name.

I reached up and rubbed the top of my head, trying to smooth the feeling away.

Something caught the light—just a flicker, a glint—and before I could even register it, Korrin’s eyes snapped to the spot. Her expression jolted—surprise, confusion, something like awe—before she forced it back down so fast it almost looked like a blink.

If I hadn’t been looking right at her, I would’ve missed it.

“How long was I out?” I asked, pretending I hadn’t seen anything. Pretending I wasn’t suddenly aware of the way the air felt structured above me.

“Long enough to arrive at your castle, Mira,” the silver man answered before any of my own people could. His voice settled into the room like it had been waiting for me to wake up.

I pushed myself upright. My legs trembled with that hollow, post‑magic weakness I hate. The glint at the edge of my vision flickered again, but I ignored it. I was too tired to deal with phantom sparkles or Korrin’s weird look.

The room steadied around me: stone, liminal light, the quiet hum of old magic woven through the walls. It reminded me of Callendor—same threshold feeling, same sense that fate was something you could inhale if you weren’t careful.

But this place… this place felt different.
Closer.
Like it recognized me.

I checked my smudge—because it always tells the truth before I do.

It expanded, slow and sure.

Home.

I had found my true home. 

But this time?
This time I was ready.

Ready in the way a cornered animal is ready—shaking, furious, too tired to run. Ready to defend my home, the place I’d found again after losing everything in it. The place where my parents died. The place that still held their echoes in the walls.

But how was I supposed to protect it?

I was weak. Korrin was too young. Mira—my Mira, with her flame magic—could maybe hold a line for a heartbeat, but her mana would gutter out long before the danger did. I knew that. I’d watched it happen before. I’d watched my parents burn themselves empty trying to protect me.

I wasn’t going to let that happen again.

A cold, electric shiver crawled up my spine. At first I thought it was fear. Then I realized it was directional—pulling upward, toward the top of my head. The air above me thickened, like it had shape, like it was waiting for something.

Then came the sound.

A soft, crystalline click—like beads knocking together.

I froze.

Another click.
Then a faint, metallic chime, as if something small and delicate had shifted in the air above me.

I reached up and brushed my hair, but my fingers didn’t land where the sensation was. Something glinted at the edge of my vision. Korrin’s eyes snapped to the spot, widening in a flash of shock before she swallowed it down.

I didn’t have time to ask.

Because the shadows around my feet moved.

Not the way shadows normally move—stretching with light, bending with bodies. No. These shadows rose. They lifted like smoke reversing direction, curling around my ankles, my calves, my waist—not touching me, but orbiting me, recognizing me.

Then something stepped out of the darkness.

Small.
Soft.
Dusk‑born.

A creature with a tiny baby mane—just a halo of starlit fluff around its neck—eyes glowing molten gold. And he was flickering. His body stuttered between shapes—sometimes lion‑cub solid, sometimes translucent dusk, sometimes a smear of shadow with paws. His baby mane pulsed like a tiny lantern trying to stay lit, each flare sending ripples of light through the shadows around us.

But his eyes—those stayed steady.

He pressed his forehead to my knee, warm and certain, and the shadows tightened around us both like a promise.

Then the archway across the room pulsed.

A demon leaned through—jagged limbs, ember‑red eyes, tasting the air like it expected fear.

It found the cub instead.

He stepped forward—small, steady, unshakably certain. His baby mane puffed to its absolute limit, glowing faintly with each flicker. His growl was young, still finding its depth, but it didn’t waver.

He wasn’t pretending to be strong.
He was strong.
Just… small.
And flickering.

The demon hesitated.

Its ember‑eyes flicked from him to me, then to the faintly glowing beads crowning my head. A hiss escaped it—thin, uneasy, almost respectful.

The cub flickered brighter—his outline sharpening, then blurring, then sharpening again. His baby mane flared like a spark catching dry tinder.

And something in me answered.

Not with flame.
Not with fear.
With darkness.

It erupted from my shadow—cold, heavy, certain—wrapping around him first, instinctively, like armor thrown over a child stepping into a storm. He didn’t flinch. He leaned into it, eyes bright, tail blinking in and out of existence in approval.

The darkness thickened, swirling around us both, forming a barrier he anchored with his small, unwavering stance. My hands shook as the magic poured out of me—raw, unshaped, but powerful in a way that felt older than my bones.

The demon recoiled.

Actually recoiled.

It stepped back—one retreat, then another—before slipping into the dark, the archway sealing behind it with a shudder that rattled dust from the ceiling.

The moment it vanished, the darkness around me wavered.
The beads dimmed.
My vision blurred.

The magic drained out of me all at once—too fast, too heavy, too much.

My knees buckled.

I didn’t even feel myself hit the floor.

But through the bond—through the smudge, through whatever thread tied us together—I felt him move.

Not in panic.
Not in fear.

In purpose.

He stepped over me, planting his tiny paws on either side of my ribs. His baby mane fluffed up again, glowing faintly with leftover dusk‑light. His whole body flickered—sometimes solid, sometimes shadow, sometimes a trembling outline—but his stance never wavered.

Someone reached for me—Korrin, maybe, or Kira—

—and he growled.

Not at them.
Not in warning.
But in promise.

He would protect me.
He would protect all of us.
Even if he was small.
Even if he was young.
Even if he flickered like a candle in a storm.
Even if he had only just been born into this world.

The castle hummed in approval.
The beads above my head chimed once, faintly.

--- 
The world returned in pieces—sound, warmth, flickering light.

I blinked.

He was still there.

The little dusk‑cub stood over me exactly as before—tiny paws braced on my ribs, baby mane puffed to its fullest, eyes glowing molten gold. His body flickered in and out, but his devotion didn’t.

He’d stayed like that.
The entire time.

He looked at me the moment my eyes opened. His baby mane flared—bright, relieved—and he pressed his forehead to my sternum with a soft, trembling chirp.

“You…” My voice cracked. “You stayed.”

He flickered brighter, tail blinking in and out of existence in excitement.

I touched his baby mane. It pulsed under my fingers—light, shadow, dusk, all trying to exist at once. He leaned into the touch like he’d been waiting for it.

Something inside me settled.

“You protected me,” I whispered. “And them.”

He puffed his baby mane even bigger, as if to say obviously.

A laugh—thin, exhausted, real—escaped me.

A name rose in my chest before I could think.
Before I could question it.
Before I could stop it.

“Flicker.”

His ears perked.
His eyes widened.
His whole body flickered in a burst of dusk‑light so bright it cast shadows across the room.

He liked it.
He accepted it.
He became it.

“Your name is Flicker,” I said, stronger this time.

He pressed his forehead to my chin, baby mane glowing like a tiny sunrise.

And for the first time since my parents died, something in me felt whole.

They—Korrin, Kira, Flicker, and even the silver man, ever stoic—looked whole. Not unhurt, not unworried, but aligned in a way I hadn’t seen before. Like the moment had stitched something between us instead of tearing it. As I caught my breath, I smiled at my family.

(I considered including the silver man in that idea, but no. Not yet. Maybe not ever. Some circles take time to widen.)

Flicker flickered brighter at the shape of my smile, his tiny baby mane puffing like he wanted to match the warmth in my chest. And for the first time in years, the world felt like something I could stand in without bracing.
I reached out for Flicker.

His tiny baby mane flattened instantly—like a startled puffball collapsing—his whole body flickering in a rapid stutter of dusk‑light. But he didn’t back away. He leaned forward, trembling with excitement he was trying very hard to hide.

I pulled him into my arms.

He let out a soft, relieved chirp and melted against me, his flickering settling into a slow, steady pulse. I rubbed him gently against the side of my head—against the faint, newly formed beads of my crown.

“That’s where you came from,” I whispered.

He chirped again—a small, resolute sound, like he was agreeing with a truth he’d always known—then wriggled upward, paws scrabbling lightly at my shoulder.

Before I could stop him, he jumped straight into my hair.

My breath caught in a startled laugh as his baby mane puffed back out, glowing faintly between the strands. He flickered in and out, little paws kneading at my scalp like he was trying to make a nest.

“Ha,” Korrin said, grinning wide, smudge bright with delight. “He seems to like your hair.”

I couldn’t help it.
I laughed—really laughed—for the first time since leaving Callendor.

And Flicker, nestled in my hair like a tiny dusk‑born crown of his own, flickered brighter in response.

So did everyone else.
