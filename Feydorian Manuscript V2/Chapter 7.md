# Chapter 2— The Fall, Part 2
## Yaereene
"Are you sure of that, Princess? Do you even know what it _means_ to fight?" 

I had made what would be a grave mistake with that. I had fallen right into the trap he had deliberately set. But I did not know to stop then. I was too blind with rage, too blind with pity for those who had died to care.

I lifted the longsword I had taken from the jewel room. It was half my size, its blade chipped. It was behind glass that I shattered in my panic in the tunnels. It used to be my great-grandfather's— the one who found Feydor and the one who made it governable only by those who bore magic. I owe my magic to him. My manifest was said to be a piece of the magic he had, his telekinesis framing his kindness. I was a carbon copy of him.
But this? This was mine. My moment— my moment to shine. 
I swung the longsword. It glinted off a invisible shield, leaving the Lich unharmed, a jolt not unlike my sister's shock.
Before this, I would have given up. My life was found in all the stories I read— some fiction, some truth, some between. But now I was to make my own. 
"Is that all you got?" He sneered, putting the trap further into my skin. 
I let my emotions go, and entered a state of constant calculation— if the Lich parried I parried, if he paused, I pushed. But no matter what I did, I couldn't win. 
"Yaereene! Stop!" My mother's panicked pleas came through this rage state. But I didn't listen. My waist length green hair whipped back and forth as the battle wore on.

At some point, I saw a pebble fly overhead towards the Lich. I didn't think much of it, but I had thrown that pebble myself, with my telekinesis. I had just manifested my magic power for the first time. 

 I paused, realizing what I had just accomplished, and dropped my focus for thr moment. I tried to throw a bigger rock, but failed. 
 That's when the Lich made his move. He shot a lightning bolt at my sister, aloof and daring. I instinctively lept off my right foot to push her down to the ground, protect her. But the Lich had anticipated this— I had left my mother unprotected. She took the brunt of the bolt, fueled by the death of some fifty *innocent* Feydorians alone. Her battle robes fell limp as her heart stopped. 
 "She's still alive." Irin said, sadly. 
 "Take this as a lesson, Princess, that I won't be stopped." The Lich snapped and disappeared. 

## Irin
 Yae was in pain. I felt the idea hit me as if the Lich's bolt had sparked to me through the queen's body. This feeling— desperation, like Yae's body had ran out of emotional charge— weighed down on me. 
I offered the princess a hug. She shook her head, and continued crying. Her tears hit me like another lightning bolt, a sharp pain. I couldn't help but cry the same way, and I crumpled. 

Queen Imelda was my rock. I loved her— how could she have left so soon? "She isn't dead," I had to remind myself. And I reminded Yae, too, of it. 

"I cannot heal her, not here." Yae replied, her sobs weighing my heart down as if rocks were piling on my thirteen–year–old heart. 

"Then why, please, can we not _move_? The cronies we saw? They might come and… hurt us." I pleaded with Yae. Something broke in Yae, and she pulled open the gate to the city. 
"Come," she said, pulling her injured mother over her shoulder, determined to save her. I marvled at her strength. It lessened the pain somewhat for me, but I had to wonder if she was as panicked as me. She probably was. 

But none of that mattered. I saw the red shirts of the Lich's men. They were closing in on us, their footsteps echoing like a promise never fufilled. I didn't want to think about the red-shirts' families— if they had any because that pained me.  I also didn't want to become one of them, lifeless, no energy, so I ran, following my sister out of the gate. I looked back, momentarily missing the home I had had for thirteen years— aflame, no saving it. I saw the dreadful face of the Lich among his men, then, filled with dread, continued on, running with no home. Running to find a place.  
## Imelda 
My head was spinning, the only thought through my head, _Save yourself, my beautiful demigoddess._ It was her turn to lead this nation, and my time has come. I choose to be strong until I was no longer needed, but after that…

The jolt of being picked up pulled me back into consciousness. 
"I don't know, Irin. We need to find _somewhere_ she can be healed." It was Yaereene, her determination to save me evident.
Only a few days after my husband made this law, the soldiers killed Irin's parents, leaving her with grief that should not be expected of an eight year old. She found the comfort in what I could give her as a parent she would never have, and what her sister could give in kindness. Still, even a found family like us couldn't fill that gap. 

The fear came again for Irin and her sister, that fear of no one to turn to, sharper than before. Yet, they ran. It was their turn now. 

After an hour on Yae's shoulder, I beckoned to her to stop. Gently she kneeled down, and set me down. 
"Yes, Mother?" She tried not to cry, yet her young heart could not bear to carry the grief of losing her one rock, and I could not fault her for this. 
"I've been… lying to you."
