# Interlude 2– Ferris and Yae, according to Danche and Imelda
## Imelda
I felt it almost the moment I ascended — that my daughters were alive. Not unscathed, not untouched, but alive. Their spirits burned stubbornly, defiantly, the way they always had. Even from the heights of divinity, I could feel that familiar pulse of them: Yaereene’s sharp steadiness, Irin’s fierce, trembling spark. They were hurting, but they were still themselves.

And yet the prophecy would not quiet. It tugged at me, insistent, like a root catching on my hoof. They would not remain steady on their own. Someone would rise to meet them, someone who would love them with a heart as steady as mine. The Tree refused to give me a name — it never did when I most wanted one — but the truth pressed against me anyway. A presence was coming. A constant. A future I could sense but not grasp.

That was the part that infuriated me. To be a goddess and still be denied the one thing a mother wants most: certainty.

“Imelda? Is everything okay?”

Danché stood before me in his stag form, radiant as ever. His antlers were draped in the red vine — the Tree’s favored herald when it wished to speak softly. I knew that plant. I had woven it into blessings, into warnings, into lullabies. Its glow curled around his tines like the Tree itself had reached out to touch him.

“I’m… all right, Danché,” I said, though the words tasted thin. “I am thinking of Yaereene and Irin. Something feels off — something I cannot quite put my fingers, or hooves, to.”

The frustration tightened my brow. Even ascended, even with the Tree’s roots threaded through my spirit, there were truths just beyond reach. I could feel the shape of what was coming for my daughters — the danger, the turning point, the heart that would steady them — but not the details. Not the name. Not the path.

It was the curse of prophecy: to know enough to worry, and never enough to rest.

But beneath the irritation, beneath the divine restraint, the love remained — fierce, unyielding, and certain. My daughters would not walk alone. Someone was already stepping toward them.

And I would feel that truth humming in my bones until the moment they met.
