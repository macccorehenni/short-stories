# Chapter 1— The Fall, Part 1

## Yaereene

It was a normal day as far as I was concerned.  My mother was on tanterhooks and grew agitated as the day went on. Maybe it was my adolescent, inefficient emotional response clouding my judgement, but I had asked her if she had seen a vision the day before. She scoffed, an answer I had learned meant that my query had reached deaf ears. I frowned, and continued what I was doing. I smiled, looking forward to sitting down and reading. Irin barged in, and called my name.

"Yaereene!" Her blue hair shone, a sign I knew meant trouble. 

"Irin?"

"Come on, Princess!"

"I cannot— my chores—"

She pouted. She was three years my junior, a bright spark I would later realize was her magic manifest about her fingers. She was cute, the reason why I had chosen to take her under my wing, and the reason I decided to ignore him.

"Fine. Have it your way. I'm done. It's no use." Irin walked away, shoulders even with her hips, a gesture of defeat. Sometimes, this young human baffled me. And frustrated me. 

This wore me down. "Wait, Irin!"

I ran after her, and as I reached the atrium, my mind distracted, I noticed something peculiar about the amount of people seeking assistance. There were normally a few people milling about, but it was full this day. Too many were seeking for some semblance of normalcy, normalcy they'd never get. I didn't think much of this fact, though, as concentrated on finding my little friend as I was. 

Sooner, I saw the blue locks of her hair, and I came beside her, and looked out over Malynor City. 

"Wow. There are so many guards." She looked in wonder at the rolling hills of the town, flush with guards. I panicked. 

## Irin

I remember distinctly the day Feydor fell. I was being my own, cantankerous self running through the castle, not a care in the world, when I noticed all the guards in the city. I had not realized that this was not normal. I knew of course that guards in Malynor had been present since whisperings of a Lich spreading his nasty teachings had reached my protectors, those I call my parents now. I hugged the Princess, because this idea bothered me. What had I missed? As I began to find the words to ask Yaereene this, her mother— the Queen— ran up. 

"Irin, Yae, come with me. We need to go."

Of course, I pushed Queen Imelda a bit. "But, _why_?"

"Let's go."

I complied, only really because she was the boss of the castle. Even King Calen often gave up control to his wife more often than not. She was smart, cunning and loving. That's what I love about her. 

And normally her voice was even. But that day, it was jagged, sharpenal laced with fear. Her normally quiet voice was loud, authoritative. 

"This way." She was scared— but of what?

I looked at the Princess. No help there. Her face was just as panicked as mine and her mother's. 

Her antlers— small at that point, yet starting to moss over— were tilted down in fear. 

I was confused more than anything— but I complied. We went to the back of the castle and entered a secret tunnel not even I knew was there. 

"We must keep moving."

"What about Father?" Yaereene's voice matched her mother's, but more rational, echoing through the stone walls. 

"He will exist. Now keep moving, my child."

We continued through this tunnel until we saw a light. Imelda opened the door and we ventured into the castle town. 

"Halt!" Unlike the red and green of the Feydorian crest, a guard approached wearing grey and red. It reminded me of fire— powerful yet deadly. "Identify yourself. All residents of Malnor must report to the castle gates."

"I'm sorry, I am new here." Imelda said, her very brief response making me more tense, as if a butter–knife couldn't have already cut through my tension. She had the foresight to cover her and Yae's royal robes and my unkempt yet flowing hair, thanks to the Gods, already figured out what was going on. "Where are the castle gates?" Her voice was very wooden, alert, in a way only a prophet like her could muster in this uncertain of times. 

"Over that way." If the guard was suspicious of our alibi, he didn't show it. I sidled over to the Princess's side and asked her if something was off. 

"I'm not sure, Irin." I noted she had the same tense look about her as me— her brain was working overtime, charting every possibilty, and even some impossibilities. I hugged her tightly, the spark of tension brewing in my fingers. 

"I'm scared, Yae. I don't want to… die."

Yaereene, atypically for her, hugged me back. "Me too, Irin. Me too."

Somehow, that relaxed our nerves, but Yae remained alert, like a wooden electric pole, her dewlap antlers showing the first signs of blooming, something neither she nor Imelda noticed. We continued to wind our way through the expansive castle city. Every time I looked up, it was chaos. Every person we passed looked stressed— they weren't sure if they'd live another day. A specific person halted our progress once again— one I would later learn the name of, one I would later fight, one I would later realize gave me my family— the Lich of Feydor. 

## Imelda

"Who are you?"

Of course I knew who he was. He was the Lich of Feydor, here to burn the island with his corrosive manner and his deadly force. But to keep my naivëty act, I decided that his name was not important. 

"More important— who are you?" He sneered, a knowing voice. But he knew not that he would be dead within the year. My time was near too. As Yaereene, Irin and I weaved through the tunnels, I had a vision. I lay in the fields, bleeding. My daughter— her antlers shining bright with the healing I knew she would need. But I also knew that the time was not _now_, neither to live nor to heal. 

"You should leave." I mustered my courage, or what was left of it. The curse of prophecy is you know everything before it happens. I wanted my life to continue, but it was not to be. 

For now I could only put on a brave face. My family depended on it. 

## Yaereene 

The Lich looked unstable. 



Not like fire, not like his underlings— his fire looked more like steam given shape. His green hair was the only thing linking him to the land he swore to destroy. His skin shone, his power of death apparent. 



How many deaths had to happen for this moment? His grey skin, sparkling in the midday, made some primal urge in me activate, made me want to run. But I did not. 



I fought, my blood boiling. I did not want my land to fall to this person who was poisonous. I was done with my mother's pacifism. I wanted to solve this here and now. 



"You will not touch my mother." My voice was unnaturally loud. I recoiled slightly at this, but continued. 



"I am the princess, and you _will not_ hurt one person in this town. My mother is too kind— she's seen who you are, yet she will not lay a hand on you. She will not say anything before she thinks about it, but me? I will."



My mother had tensed up when I said that. I didn't care. I was done hiding from this. I wanted him gone. In retrospect though, that was the downfall of Feydor. Not the Lich, my rash decision. He finished what I started. 

"I will fight, if that's what it comes down to. You will be no more."
