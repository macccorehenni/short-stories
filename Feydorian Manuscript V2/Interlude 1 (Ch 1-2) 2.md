# Interlude 1— The Story of Yae
## Danché

Yaereene is my daughter, and Imelda is my patron. I chose the Queen not for her power, but for her candor. Her honesty. The rot that consumed Feydor could never touch her. That is why I chose her.
I had watched her for many seasons before I chose. A devout soul, she often could be found under the Tree of Prophecy. The tree was older than the Island's memory, its bark furrowed with memories of those who asked its counsel.
 Imelda tended to the tree, one which had seen  the old, invisible conflict of *who belonged*, the long struggle of *who was meant to remain*. She gave it her mana, and in return it gave her its ancient magic. The tree mirrored who Imelda was: steadfast, unwavering, and unwilling to forgo its kindness. 
And so I chose the half-elf to bear my blessing, for she alone remembered what the Island remembered, a sanctuary for those who were adrift. 
The night before the birth of the half-elf, I stepped forth once more before my patron. 
"Imelda, within you is a child. This child is not merely a heir to your throne, nor is it a curse meant to test you. An old evil stirs, and only the kindness you carry can soften its edge.
"Your kindness is not a weakness. Through your child, that mercy will take form— not only in Feydor, but in realms yet untraveled by any Feydorian. 
Have faith, and raise her well."

Imelda smiled at first, not yet understanding the full weight of my words. She bowed deeply, as she had before. Then understanding struck, and her smile faded into a deep reverence. 

"I will do that." Her smile returned as she readied herself to bear her child, a calm settling over her as she realized the grace I had given her. 

Sixteen years passed, and my intuition was proved true. Together, the King and Queen of Feydor raised the heart of the Island, and the Queen deepened her prophetic sight with each day she turned her prayers towards me. 

The day before the Island fell, I came to her once more, this time to tell her something I could not hold back— the fact that I wished to raise her to divinity. 

 I had watched her grow alongside her child,  rising into a leader her people trusted without hesitation. Even her husband, the King of Feydor, deferred to the quiet wisdom and the kind yet steady judgment and far‑seeing insight that had become her hallmark.
This selflessness showed that she was the true savior the Island needed— her spirit shaped for eternity, a heart too vast for her mortal body. 

As her final breath slipped free, ascension met her gently,  for her time upon the Material Plane had reached its rightful end.  

And as my daughter stepped forward to take her mother’s place, I smiled upon them both — for I know the resting place of women like Imelda, and the path awaiting women like Yaereene.
